Before you start

  • Read Passage 1 carefully first — note the writer's line of argument paragraph by paragraph.

  • Own words means own words. No marks for straight lifts.
  • Analysis = quotation + comment. A quotation on its own earns nothing.
  • Mark honestly. Only tick a marking point if your answer makes it clearly.

How Higher English is assessed from session 2026–27, and how to answer every type of question. Based on the Qualifications Scotland Higher English course specification (v6.0, September 2025), the changes to the Scottish text section published on 3 June 2026, and the specimen question papers.

The course assessment

Reading for Understanding, Analysis & Evaluation 30%
30 marks · 1 hour 30 minutes · two unseen non-fiction passages linked by theme. Questions on Passage 1, then one 5-mark question on both passages.
Critical Reading 40%
40 marks · 1 hour 45 minutes. Section 1: Scottish text (20). Section 2: critical essay (20). A different genre in each section, and never the same text twice.
Portfolio–writing 30%
ONE piece, broadly creative or broadly discursive, marked out of 15 and doubled to 30. Max 1,300 words (a penalty applies if more than 10% over). No minimum.
Spoken language
Achieved / not achieved — but you must achieve it to get the course award. Group discussion and/or a presentation where you answer audience questions.
2027 exam date
Friday 7 May 2027 (morning): RUAE 09:00–10:30, Critical Reading 11:00–12:45. Source: Qualifications Scotland 2027 timetable (v02, August 2026).
What changed for 2026–27
Scottish text: the final question drops from 10 to 8 marks (2 commonality + 2 extract + 4 elsewhere) and the extract questions rise from 10 to 12 marks. The paper is still 40 marks. Nothing else in the course changed.

RUAE question types

Understanding — "use your own words"
No marks for straight lifts. Find the key ideas and gloss them. Usually 1 mark per clear point; "explain fully" needs developed points.
Analysis — "analyse how…"
Quote + comment. 2 marks for a detailed/insightful comment, 1 for a basic one, 0 for a quotation alone. "At least two examples" = 2+2, 2+1+1 or 1+1+1+1.
Word choice
Quote the word → its connotations → what that shows about the writer's ideas or attitude.
Imagery
Literal root of the image → how it applies to the subject → the effect. "Just as a tsunami…, so the complaints…"
Sentence structure
Lists, repetition, short/minor sentences, questions, inversion, climax/anticlimax, parallel structure — always tied to WHAT is being emphasised.
Punctuation
Colon (explanation/list) · semicolon (balance/contrast) · dashes (aside/dramatic addition) · inverted commas (sarcasm) · ellipsis (pause/omission).
Tone
Name it precisely (sarcastic, nostalgic, dismissive…) and analyse the language that creates it.
Link / structure
Show the words that refer BACK to the previous idea and the words that introduce the NEW idea. "Function" questions ask what job a paragraph does.
Evaluation
Analyse, then judge effectiveness — e.g. how a conclusion echoes ideas or images from the opening.
Final question — 5 marks
Three key areas of agreement (or disagreement) supported from BOTH passages. 3 areas = 3; with appropriate evidence = 4; with detailed/insightful evidence = 5. Bullet points are allowed.

Critical Reading — Section 1: Scottish text

What you get
An extract (or a whole poem) from the set text you studied, with questions worth 12 marks, then a final 8-mark question.
Extract questions
Like RUAE analysis: quotation + comment on language, characterisation, mood, theme. 2 marks for detailed comment, 1 for basic, 0 for quotation alone.
Final question — 8 marks
2 commonality (what links the extract to the rest of the text, in terms of the question) · 2 the extract · 4 elsewhere in the text/other poems or stories.
How to revise
For each theme of your text, learn 4–6 short quotations from DIFFERENT parts of the text (or different poems). Practise writing commonality statements.

Critical Reading — Section 2: critical essay

The task
Choose one question (from a choice of three in your genre) and write an essay on a text you have studied — drama, prose, poetry, film & TV drama or language. Must be a different genre from Section 1.
Structure
Introduction (title, writer, key words of the question, line of thought) → 4–6 PEAE paragraphs → conclusion that evaluates the text as a whole.
Marking bands
20–19 · 18–16 · 15–13 · 12–10 · 9–6 · 5–0. Judged on knowledge & understanding, analysis and evaluation.
Key band words
Analysis: perceptive → detailed → clear → adequate → limited. Evaluation: committed → engaged → clear → adequate.
Technical accuracy
Few errors in spelling, grammar, sentence construction, punctuation and paragraphing; understood at first reading. If not met (or irrelevant), maximum 9 marks.
Timing
About 45–50 minutes, including 5 minutes to plan. Keep every paragraph tied to the question's wording.

Portfolio–writing

Broadly creative
Personal/reflective essay · short story or episode · poem or linked poems · drama script. Top band: strong creative qualities, skilful command of genre, mature reflection, strong sense of personality.
Broadly discursive
Argumentative or persuasive essay · report · informative writing. Top band: skilful research and selection, strong sustained line of thought, convincing stance.
Rules
First draft in class under supervision (up to 4 hours). Teacher feedback on ONE draft only. Acknowledge all sources. Using generative AI for anything other than suggesting source material is plagiarism.
Marks
Bands 15–13 · 12–10 · 9–7 · 6–4 · 3–1 · 0. Consistent technical accuracy is needed for 9–7 and above.

Exam tips

  • Quote, then comment. In RUAE and the Scottish text, a quotation on its own earns 0 marks.
  • Own words. In understanding questions, no marks for straight lifts.
  • Answer the question asked. Use its key words in every essay paragraph.
  • Know the 8-mark split. Scottish text final question: 2 commonality, 2 extract, 4 elsewhere (from 2026–27).
  • Different genres. Your critical essay must be a different genre from your Scottish text.
  • Watch the clock. Leave 15 minutes for the RUAE final question; split Critical Reading roughly in half.
  • Accuracy matters. An essay with significant technical errors is capped at 9 out of 20.
